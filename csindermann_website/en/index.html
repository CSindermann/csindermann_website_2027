Research interests

Person-Environment Interactions in Online Contexts

I explore how different technologies change how information is created, distributed, filtered, organized, and finally presented to individuals online.

Additionally, I investigate how individuals with different characteristics co-produce and cognitively process information presented to them online and, building on that, form and change their political views and behaviors.

Phenomena like filter bubbles, echo chambers, disinformation, AI-propaganda, and LLM-mediated discourse as well as topics such as polarization, radicalization online, and online political participation are central to this work.

Building on my basic research, I also conduct applied research to support the development of empirically validated interventions that support individuals in forming informed and, as far as possible, unbiased views in the online world.

To conduct this research, I apply an inter- and transdisciplinary research approach combining expertise from various research fields and stakeholders. Moreover, I use various methods from surveys and experiments to the analysis of digital trace data by means of machine learning algorithms.
